In development

Prove you're old enough — without proving who you are.

EroPass is a device-bound, privacy-first age verification system. It's built to satisfy age-gating requirements for online content without forcing people to repeatedly upload ID, or forcing platforms to become custodians of sensitive identity data.

The core product is EroPassID, an iOS app that checks a person's age once, then holds a reusable, cryptographically signed proof of age on their phone that can be presented to participating websites on demand.

01 The problem we're solving

Age-verification laws for online content are expanding quickly, and most implementations force an uncomfortable choice: sites either do nothing, or they collect and store government ID scans, selfies, and birthdates directly — creating large, centralized troves of sensitive identity data that become high-value targets for breaches, leaks, and misuse.

EroPass exists to remove that trade-off. Verification should happen once, be portable, and reveal nothing more than the minimum fact required: is this person old enough, yes or no.

02 What EroPass does

EroPass separates the age check from proof of age. A person confirms their age once, using a government-issued ID already stored in Apple Wallet on their iPhone. EroPass then issues a time-limited, cryptographically signed credential bound to that person's device. Sites that need to gate age-restricted content can ask for proof of that credential — and get a signed yes/no answer — without ever learning the person's name, birthdate, or seeing their identity documents.

This is designed for use by adult content platforms, age-gated marketplaces, and any online service with a legal or policy obligation to verify age before granting access.

03 How it works

This describes the current development build. Apple has granted EroPass access to the age check in step 1 for development only; approval for public release has not yet been granted.
1
One-time age check

The EroPassID app asks the ID in your Apple Wallet for one fact: that you are 18 or over. Your iPhone shows you what is being requested, and nothing is sent until you approve it. EroPass does not ask for your name, date of birth, photo, address or document number, and never receives an image of your ID.

2
Device-bound credential

On success, the app creates a private key inside your iPhone's Secure Enclave and receives a signed age credential tied to that key. The credential says only that you are 18 or over, the day it was issued, and when it expires. The private key never leaves the device.

3
Presenting proof of age

When a participating page needs to confirm age, it shows a QR code. You scan it with EroPassID and approve with Face ID; your phone signs a one-time challenge with its key. The page learns only "verified" or "not verified."

4
Expiry & renewal

Credentials expire (currently after 30 days). Renewing means repeating the age check, so a stale credential can't be used indefinitely.

5
No record of use

EroPass's server checks each scan, so it handles your credential and your device's public key for the moment of that check. It stores neither, and keeps no record that links one scan to another, to a device, or to a person.

04 Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 24, 2026  ·  Applies to: EroPass, the EroPassID application, and eropass.com

EroPass is not yet available to the public. The EroPassID app has not been released, and the EroPass service is reachable only by our own test devices. This policy describes how the current development build handles data. Where something is not yet decided or not yet built, it says so. It will be updated before public release.

Who we are

EroPass is developed by Paradigm Net Media ("EroPass," "we," "us," or "our"). This policy explains what information EroPass handles, why, and the choices you have.

Our privacy commitment

EroPass is built on a data-minimization principle: we collect and retain the least information necessary to issue and check a proof-of-age credential, and no more. Your identity documents stay in your wallet and never reach EroPass's servers.

This website

eropass.com is a single static page. It sets no cookies and uses no analytics, trackers, advertising or forms. The page is hosted on GitHub Pages, so GitHub receives your IP address in order to deliver it, under GitHub's privacy statement. We do not receive those logs.

Information the EroPass service handles

  • Your age check: your iPhone sends us an encrypted response from the ID in your Apple Wallet. It contains the single answer we asked for (18 or over) together with the cryptographic material needed to confirm it came from a genuine ID, such as the issuing authority's signature and certificate. We check it in memory and discard it. The response itself is never stored or logged. The only record is the event log line described below: that an age check passed or was refused, and a general reason category.
  • Device-bound key: EroPassID creates a private signing key inside your device's Secure Enclave. The private key is never sent to EroPass or anyone else. Its public half is sent to us when your credential is issued and each time you scan, is used for that check, and is not stored.
  • Age credential: issued to your phone and kept only there. We hold no copy and no list of credentials.
  • Browser cookie: when you open an age-gated page that uses EroPass, we set one strictly necessary cookie containing a random value, so the page can show its own result. It is not used for tracking or analytics and expires after 30 minutes.
  • Session data: a random session id (shown in the QR code), one-time numbers that prevent replay, and a "verified" flag for that session. None of these is linked to you or your device. Lifetimes are listed under "Data retention."
  • Event logs: for each event our application logs the time, the type of event, an outcome category and the path requested. A line has no id for the individual request and contains no IP address, browser details, session id or device identifier.
  • No web server request log: our web server does not log individual requests.
  • Network delivery: requests reach us through Cloudflare, which delivers our traffic and blocks abuse. Cloudflare decrypts each request before passing it to us, so as well as your IP address it is able to read what your phone and browser send us and what we send back, including your age credential and device public key when you scan, the session id and the browser cookie. The one exception is the age check response from the ID in your Apple Wallet, which is separately encrypted to a key that only we hold, so Cloudflare cannot read it. Cloudflare handles this data under its own privacy policy.

We do not collect

  • Your name
  • Government ID photos or numbers
  • Home address
  • Date of birth
  • A device fingerprint or advertising identifier
  • A stored record of where or when you use your credential
  • Biometric data — Face ID never leaves your device

We do handle (minimally)

  • The "18 or over" answer, checked and discarded
  • One short-lived cookie and random session data
  • Event logs with no IP address, session id or device identifier

The age check and Apple

The age check uses Apple's Verify with Wallet. The request and your approval happen on your iPhone, in Apple's own interface, and the app declares to the system that EroPass will not store what it receives. Apple's handling of your ID in Wallet is covered by Apple's privacy policy.

Apple has granted EroPass access to this feature for development only. Approval for public release has not yet been granted. EroPass has no other verification method. EroPass is not affiliated with or endorsed by Apple.

How we use information

Information is used solely to: issue, check, and expire age credentials; maintain the security and integrity of the system; prevent fraud and abuse; and comply with applicable law.

  • Your age credential is not kept by us. We hold no copy, no list of credentials and no record of the device it went to.
  • The event log is kept on our own server. No fixed retention period has been set for it during development. One will be stated here before public release.

On your device

  • A signing key held in the Secure Enclave. It can be used only after Face ID, and it stops working if the Face ID enrollment on the phone changes.
  • Your age credential, which states only that you are 18 or over, the day it was issued, when it expires, and your device's public key. It is valid for a limited period (currently 30 days).
  • A Face ID change marker: a value iOS provides that changes when Face ID enrollment changes, so the app can ask for a new age check. It is not biometric data and cannot be used to recognize anyone.

All three are stored for this device only. They are not synced to iCloud and cannot be restored onto a different phone.

There is not yet an in-app control to delete them; one is planned before public release. Deleting the app alone may not remove them, because iOS can keep an app's secure storage after the app is removed. Erasing the iPhone removes them.

Data sharing

We do not sell personal data. A page that uses EroPass receives only "verified" or "not verified." We may disclose limited information if required to comply with applicable law, regulation, or legal process; because of how EroPass is built, we hold very little that could be disclosed.

Children's privacy

EroPass is not directed at children, and its entire purpose is to help prevent access to age-restricted content by people who are underage. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If an age check does not confirm that the person is 18 or over, no credential is issued and the response is discarded. What we keep from that attempt is one event log line: the time, that an age check was refused, and a general reason category (for example, that the answer was not "18 or over"). That line contains no IP address, session id or device identifier.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, or request deletion of personal information we hold about you. Because EroPass keeps no account, no identity data and no lasting record of your age check, there is usually nothing on our servers that can be tied to you. To ask a question or exercise these rights, contact us using the details below.

Security

EroPass uses device-level hardware security (a key held in the Secure Enclave that requires Face ID for each use), cryptographic signing (ECDSA) for credentials, and encryption in transit (TLS) for all network communication. We never receive ID documents or images, so there are none to store.
